# 0080. Drawings for the book's themes

Status: accepted (2026-11-20). Amends 0073 and 0077.
Phase: P1

## Context

The owner quoted a reader on what the novel is: its descriptions "of nature in its decline, the fading wolves, the evocation of Mexico, man's cruelty to man and insensitivity to others' suffering", and called that its essence. Two readers went through all 150 drawings against those four themes, reading every chapter. Mexico was carried well. The fading wolves were strong in Part I and absent from Parts III and IV. Nature in decline was almost missing. Cruelty appeared mostly as its aftermath, and no drawing said indifference outright. About a dozen drawings were near-identical riding scenes. The owner adopted all 50 proposals.

## Decision

- **Sixteen captions and notes carry a theme** without a new picture, for example "The pit and its witnesses", "She-wolf, out of a cut-over Sonora", "No more wolves", "Ten centavos to see her", "The world rolled on" and "Driven off in the rain". Each was checked against its passage, in our own words.
- **Sixteen drawings are redrawn in place** to show their theme: the crowd on the bleachers at the pit, stumps behind the she-wolf, Billy with the pipe as the crippled dog hobbles off, the turned-back mattress in the Parham house, a meadow of stumps by the silver mine, the dead she-wolf among the drowned stars, the nameless cross on Boyd's grave and others.
- **Eleven weak or repeated drawings are replaced** near the same place, and one riding scene is dropped: the woman at prayer for her dead sons at San Buenaventura, the bandolero's dawn ambush, the fair in the alameda with its Apache boys, the old woman's tale of the shot priest, the cairns of the slain on a dead pass, and six more.
- **Six drawings are new** where a theme had nothing: the granja wall in the blind man's story, the girl taken away in Quijada's telling, Billy's dream of the wolves, the slab lumber at Las Varas, the slag heaps at Corralitos and the die-up Sanders remembers.
- **Told stories are framed as told.** Drawings of a story someone tells, rather than a scene Billy sees, stand in a broadside border as the corrido's do, and their captions say whose telling it is.
- **The no-touching rule (decision 0077) is checked every half zoom**, not every whole zoom: a drawing's size changes between zoom steps, and one pair touched at zoom 10.5 alone.

## Alternatives

- **Captions only.** The cheapest change, but the pit with no crowd and the Parham house from outside cannot show their themes in words alone.
- **Add the new scenes and keep every old drawing.** The map would pass 170 drawings and keep the repetitive riding scenes the review found.

## Consequences

The map has 155 drawings, first showing at zoom 7 (2), 8 (18), 9 (55), 10 (39), 11 (31) and 12 (10). None touches another at any quarter zoom from 7 to 14. Some redrawn details read only at the larger sizes (the coyote tied behind Billy's saddle, the hat in the girl's hand at the granja wall); their captions carry them at zoom 9.
